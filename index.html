Designing a Sense of Calm

My work for Wipro Seating Solutions spans multiple collections and communication pieces, with the launch of the Dyān Collection being one of the key projects I worked on.

Rooted in the idea of aligning body and mind, Dyān is designed around human-centricity, refined aesthetics and intelligent ergonomics. Taking inspiration from this philosophy, I wanted the visual communication surrounding the collection to evoke the same feeling as the furniture itself, calm, balanced and intentional.

Rather than creating overly busy compositions, I conceptualized the designs around a sense of visual stillness. Soft environments, restrained layouts, thoughtful use of space and subtle lighting allowed the chairs and their sculptural forms to become the focal point. The intention was to create imagery that almost feels like a pause, communicating comfort and mindfulness before the product is even experienced physically.

Every element was considered to reinforce the larger philosophy behind Dyān: furniture that doesn't simply occupy a space, but contributes to how that space feels.

Beyond Dyān, I have also created designs for other Wipro Seating Solutions collections, adapting the visual language to different products and communication requirements while maintaining the brand's emphasis on thoughtful, human-centred design.

Making Gold Feel Different

My work with Youlry, an 18K pure gold jewellery brand, extended across social media design, campaign concepts and art direction for shoots. The objective was to move beyond creating beautiful individual posts and begin building a more cohesive and recognisable visual language for the brand.

Youlry’s jewellery is contemporary, expressive and often playful, and I wanted its communication to carry that same personality. I approached each design with a concept-first mindset, finding unexpected ways to place jewellery at the centre of a story — from fashion and pop-culture references to games, surreal environments and larger-than-life interpretations of the pieces themselves.

Larger Than Life

The Larger Than Life series was designed to make the jewellery impossible to ignore. By exaggerating the scale of each piece and turning it into part of the model’s environment, I created surreal, playful compositions that visually bring the line “I’d get distracted by the jewellery too” to life.

The oversized forms shift the jewellery from an accessory into the hero of the frame, while the rich burgundy palette, dramatic proportions and clean execution retain the premium feel of 18K gold. The result is a campaign that feels bold, playful and unexpected, while staying rooted in Youlry’s contemporary visual language

While the concepts could be playful, the execution always needed to feel premium. Rich tones, considered compositions, dramatic lighting and clean layouts helped create a balance between imagination and sophistication, allowing the 18K gold jewellery to remain the hero of every visual.

A key part of my approach was also creating a stronger rhythm across Youlry’s social presence. Rather than every creative existing in isolation, I worked towards a visual world that could evolve from campaign to campaign while still feeling distinctly Youlry.

FOR CONCLUSIONS

Beyond digital design, being involved in art direction for shoots allowed me to carry this thinking into photography as well, considering styling, composition, mood and how each image would ultimately live within the brand’s larger visual ecosystem.

The result was a body of work that aimed to make fine jewellery feel less conventional and more editorial, imaginative and culturally relevant, without losing the sense of refinement that comes with 18K gold.

Wewe Uzuri

A self-initiated editorial project exploring wildlife, conservation and indigenous communities across Africa, bringing together powerful photography and thoughtful storytelling to celebrate the relationship between people, wildlife and the natural world.

Born from my personal love for wildlife, WEWE UZURI became an opportunity to experiment with editorial design without the boundaries of a client brief. I explored typography, colour, pacing and image-led layouts to give each story its own mood, while creating a cohesive visual journey across the publication.

Client
Passion Project
Role
Editorial Design & Layout
Deliverables

Software

Where Curiosity Became a Publication

Born from my love for wildlife, nature and the stories that exist within it, this editorial project began without a client or a brief, simply as an opportunity to explore what would happen if I translated a subject I deeply enjoy into a complete visual publication.

I designed the magazine as an exercise in editorial storytelling, exploring how photography, typography, colour and negative space could work together to create rhythm from one spread to the next. Rather than following a rigid visual template, I allowed the subject of each story to influence its design, moving between immersive full-bleed imagery, quieter text-led pages and more expressive compositions.

For the Tribes of Africa feature, I wanted the documentary photography to remain at the heart of the story. Large, immersive images are balanced with quieter text-led compositions, allowing the layouts to feel informative without taking away from the people, environments and narratives being documented.

The visual language shifts with the tone of each story, moving from black and muted gold to deep forest greens and earthy tones drawn from the landscapes themselves. Rather than forcing every spread into the same structure, I allowed the layouts, typography and colour palette to adapt with the content, creating a more immersive and sensitive editorial journey through the stories of the BaAka and Baka peoples.

More than anything, this project gave me the freedom to experiment with pace and visual hierarchy — knowing when a photograph should command an entire spread and when the design should pull back to let the story speak.

It became a meeting point between two things I love: design and the natural world, and an exploration of how thoughtful editorial design can turn information into something that feels immersive, emotive and worth slowing down for.

Branding & Identity Redesign

Tata Consultancy Services

A visual identity exploration for TCS, created to bring a more contemporary, dynamic and expressive layer to an established global brand.

The approach began with a simple idea: looking within the existing TCS identity for a visual element that could be expanded into an entire design system. The curves within the TCS logo became the starting point, evolving into fluid lines, forms and patterns that could move freely across different compositions.

Paired with bold gradients, vibrant colour and generous white space, the system was designed to feel modern and distinctive without losing the clarity and professionalism of the brand. Most importantly, it was built to be flexible, adapting seamlessly across print, stationery, editorial layouts and digital applications.

Client
Tata Consultancy Services (TCS)
Role
Lead Brand Designer
Deliverables
Brand Identity, Print Collateral, Digital Assets
Software
Photoshop

The Flow of Intelligence

The visual language is built around movement and continuity. Inspired by the curves of the TCS logo, flowing lines become a recurring graphic device, bending, intersecting and travelling through compositions to create a sense of progress.

A palette of vivid blues, warm oranges and soft gradients adds depth and energy, while clean typography and restrained layouts keep the system grounded. Together, these elements create an identity that feels technological yet human, structured yet constantly in motion.

Extending the Aesthetic to Physical Objects

The identity was designed as a system rather than a single visual treatment, allowing the same core elements to adapt across a wide range of physical applications.

From letterheads and business cards to merchandise and corporate stationery, the fluid lines and gradients shift in scale and composition while remaining instantly connected. This flexibility allows each touchpoint to feel unique while still belonging to one cohesive visual world.

Corporate Card Mockup Variations

Business card explorations translate the new visual system into a smaller, more functional format. Minimal typography and generous negative space keep information clear, while cropped gradients and flowing forms introduce personality without overpowering the content.

The Redesign Monograph

The result demonstrates how the system can scale from subtle corporate communication to more expressive editorial compositions, without losing its underlying visual consistency.

Campaign & OOH Design

Godrej Homes

A campaign pitch for Godrej Properties, exploring how its upcoming residential development in Kharadi, Pune could be positioned as a space where architecture, nature and everyday living exist in harmony.

Built around the thought “In Tune With Life,” the visual direction uses rhythmic architectural forms, dramatic natural light and moments of stillness to create an elevated yet inviting world. Across outdoor and digital formats, the campaign moves away from conventional real-estate imagery to focus on something more emotional, not simply what a home looks like, but how it could feel to live there.

Client
Godrej Properties
Role
Senior Graphic Designer
Deliverables
Billboard Design, Social Media, OOH
Software
Photoshop, Ai

Architecture in Rhythm

For the outdoor campaign, architecture became the hero. Arches, corridors, staircases and repeating structural forms were used to create a visual sense of rhythm, translating the idea of being “In Tune With Life” into the compositions themselves.

Rather than relying on conventional property imagery, the layouts use scale, symmetry, shadow and open space to create intrigue from a distance. Restrained typography allows the imagery to command attention, while glimpses of blue sky and natural light introduce a feeling of openness beyond the architecture.

Metro Transit Highlight

The transit adaptation shifts the campaign from dramatic architecture towards warmth, light and interior living. Set against the constant movement of a metro environment, the serene composition creates a deliberate moment of contrast, a glimpse into a home that feels calm, open and full of light.

A Pause in the Rush

For the transit environment, the campaign was imagined as a visual pause within the movement of the city. Warm interiors, soft daylight, arched openings and natural elements create an immediate contrast to the pace and structure of everyday commuting.

Each execution explores a different expression of the same idea, light, warmth, balance and room to breathe, allowing the campaign to remain visually varied while belonging to one cohesive world.

Rather than communicating luxury through excess, the direction presents it through space, serenity and considered simplicity.

Extending the Story Digitally

The campaign language was extended into vertical social formats, allowing the concept to become more intimate and atmospheric.

Here, sunlight, shadows, architectural repetition and natural textures become storytelling devices. Each frame captures a small sensory moment, light passing through a curtain, shadows traveling across an arcade, or the evening sun entering through an arch.

Editorial Screen Layouts

Vertical compositions designed to carry the campaign’s architectural rhythm, light and sense of movement into digital-first formats.

The Warmth of Home

The second social direction shifts the focus from architecture to the feeling of being home.

Golden-hour light, warm materials and expansive interiors create spaces that feel lived-in rather than staged. The recurring arch motif connects the lifestyle imagery back to the broader campaign language, while framing different moments of comfort, nature and city living.

The result is a softer expression of the campaign, presenting the development not simply as a property, but as a place where life has more room to unfold.

Interior Lifestyle Moments

Warm, light-filled interiors exploring a quieter and more emotional side of the campaign, where comfort, openness and everyday living take centre stage.
