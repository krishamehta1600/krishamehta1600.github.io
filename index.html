Designing a Sense of Calm

My work for Wipro Seating Solutions spans multiple collections and communication pieces, with the launch of the Dyān Collection being one of the key projects I worked on.

Rooted in the idea of aligning body and mind, Dyān is designed around human-centricity, refined aesthetics and intelligent ergonomics. Taking inspiration from this philosophy, I wanted the visual communication surrounding the collection to evoke the same feeling as the furniture itself, calm, balanced and intentional.

Rather than creating overly busy compositions, I conceptualized the designs around a sense of visual stillness. Soft environments, restrained layouts, thoughtful use of space and subtle lighting allowed the chairs and their sculptural forms to become the focal point. The intention was to create imagery that almost feels like a pause, communicating comfort and mindfulness before the product is even experienced physically.

Every element was considered to reinforce the larger philosophy behind Dyān: furniture that doesn't simply occupy a space, but contributes to how that space feels.

Beyond Dyān, I have also created designs for other Wipro Seating Solutions collections, adapting the visual language to different products and communication requirements while maintaining the brand's emphasis on thoughtful, human-centred design.

Making Gold Feel Different

My work with Youlry, an 18K pure gold jewellery brand, extended across social media design, campaign concepts and art direction for shoots. The objective was to move beyond creating beautiful individual posts and begin building a more cohesive and recognisable visual language for the brand.

Youlry’s jewellery is contemporary, expressive and often playful, and I wanted its communication to carry that same personality. I approached each design with a concept-first mindset, finding unexpected ways to place jewellery at the centre of a story — from fashion and pop-culture references to games, surreal environments and larger-than-life interpretations of the pieces themselves.

Larger Than Life

The Larger Than Life series was designed to make the jewellery impossible to ignore. By exaggerating the scale of each piece and turning it into part of the model’s environment, I created surreal, playful compositions that visually bring the line “I’d get distracted by the jewellery too” to life.

The oversized forms shift the jewellery from an accessory into the hero of the frame, while the rich burgundy palette, dramatic proportions and clean execution retain the premium feel of 18K gold. The result is a campaign that feels bold, playful and unexpected, while staying rooted in Youlry’s contemporary visual language

While the concepts could be playful, the execution always needed to feel premium. Rich tones, considered compositions, dramatic lighting and clean layouts helped create a balance between imagination and sophistication, allowing the 18K gold jewellery to remain the hero of every visual.

A key part of my approach was also creating a stronger rhythm across Youlry’s social presence. Rather than every creative existing in isolation, I worked towards a visual world that could evolve from campaign to campaign while still feeling distinctly Youlry.

FOR CONCLUSIONS

Beyond digital design, being involved in art direction for shoots allowed me to carry this thinking into photography as well, considering styling, composition, mood and how each image would ultimately live within the brand’s larger visual ecosystem.

The result was a body of work that aimed to make fine jewellery feel less conventional and more editorial, imaginative and culturally relevant, without losing the sense of refinement that comes with 18K gold.

Where Curiosity Became a Publication

Born from my love for wildlife, nature and the stories that exist within it, this editorial project began without a client or a brief, simply as an opportunity to explore what would happen if I translated a subject I deeply enjoy into a complete visual publication.

I designed the magazine as an exercise in editorial storytelling, exploring how photography, typography, colour and negative space could work together to create rhythm from one spread to the next. Rather than following a rigid visual template, I allowed the subject of each story to influence its design, moving between immersive full-bleed imagery, quieter text-led pages and more expressive compositions.

For the Tribes of Africa feature, I wanted the documentary photography to remain at the heart of the story. Large, immersive images are balanced with quieter text-led compositions, allowing the layouts to feel informative without taking away from the people, environments and narratives being documented.

The visual language shifts with the tone of each story, moving from black and muted gold to deep forest greens and earthy tones drawn from the landscapes themselves. Rather than forcing every spread into the same structure, I allowed the layouts, typography and colour palette to adapt with the content, creating a more immersive and sensitive editorial journey through the stories of the BaAka and Baka peoples.

More than anything, this project gave me the freedom to experiment with pace and visual hierarchy — knowing when a photograph should command an entire spread and when the design should pull back to let the story speak.

It became a meeting point between two things I love: design and the natural world, and an exploration of how thoughtful editorial design can turn information into something that feels immersive, emotive and worth slowing down for.

The Flow of Intelligence

Knorr

A Curiosity-Led Launch Concept for Knorr Korean

A campaign proposal developed for the launch of Knorr’s Korean range, exploring how the brand could tap into the growing interest around Korean food and culture in India.

The central idea used Korean typography as a curiosity trigger, proposing billboards that audiences might instinctively stop to decode or translate. Supported by bold food imagery, Knorr’s familiar brand colours and Korean-inspired graphic elements, the concept aimed to turn the unfamiliar into an invitation to discover something new.

Client
Knorr (Unilever)
Role
Concept Development & Visual Design
Project type
Campaign Proposal / Pitch
Software
Photoshop

What Does That Say?

The proposed campaign began with a simple behavioural insight: when we see something we don’t immediately understand, curiosity makes us want to figure it out.

Instead of leading with conventional product messaging, the billboards would feature prominent Korean copy, encouraging passers-by to pause, scan or translate it to uncover the message.

The reveal would lead back to Knorr’s Korean range, turning the act of translation into part of the brand experience and creating a playful introduction to Korean flavours.

A series of billboard directions were developed to explore how the idea could come alive across the city.

The layouts experimented with Korean-led typography, bold product and food imagery, graphic patterns and contrasting colour systems. Black offered a more contemporary, culture-led expression, while red and green created stronger connections to Knorr’s existing visual identity.

Rather than proposing one fixed execution, these explorations demonstrated how the central concept could evolve while remaining recognisable as part of the same campaign world.

Mainstream Appeal

The packaging line took the bold red and green colour profile, along with other vibrant colours to supermarket shelves. Featuring high-energy limited edition Squid Game co-branding, the design targeted Gen-Z consumers eager for authentic culinary adventures.

In digital channels, micro-content highlighted simple, quick recipe integrations, translating complex traditional preparation steps into accessible, everyday kitchen hacks.

Curiosity Before Communication

At the heart of the proposal was the idea that advertising doesn’t always need to explain everything immediately.

By allowing Korean typography to become the first point of interaction, the concept explored how curiosity itself could become a communication tool — inviting audiences to participate before introducing the product.

While the proposed campaign was ultimately not taken forward into execution, the concept was well received by the client and became an exploration of how Knorr could introduce its Korean range through a more unexpected, culturally relevant and participation-led approach.

AMARULA REIMAGINED

Meet the mind Resume LinkedIn
Self-Initiated Branding & Packaging

Amarula
Reimagined

A self-initiated rebranding exploration inspired by my love for South Africa and the story behind Amarula, a spirit rooted in the marula fruit, its landscape, and the elephants that have become synonymous with the brand.

The project explores how Amarula’s identity could be reimagined for a more contemporary audience while preserving the warmth, character and distinctly African story at its core.

Rather than developing a single solution, I explored two contrasting visual directions, one expressive, warm and contemporary; the other bold, graphic and rooted in a more traditional African-inspired colour language. Both reinterpret the same brand story through different visual lenses.

Role
Brand & Packaging Design
Project type
Passion Project / Conceptual Rebrand
Deliverables
Brand Identity, Packaging, Box Design
Tools
Illustrator, Photoshop

Rooted in the Marula Story

What drew me to Amarula was the world surrounding the product, the marula fruit, the South African landscape and the elephants that naturally gather around marula trees during harvest season.

Rather than treating these elements as supporting details, I brought them to the forefront of the identity. Elephants, organic forms, marula-inspired shapes and rich colour became the building blocks of a visual system that celebrates the origin of the drink while giving it a more expressive contemporary personality.

From Bottle to Box

The visual language extends beyond the bottle into a complete packaging system. Repeating elephant motifs become patterns across the outer box, while blocks of pink, maroon and yellow create strong shelf presence and allow the packaging to feel recognisable from every angle.

The box was designed not simply as a container, but as an extension of the storytelling, creating a richer and more immersive experience around the product.

A Contemporary African Bloom

The first direction moves away from the expected earthy palette and introduces deep brown, vibrant pink and warm golden yellow for a more unexpected expression of the brand.

The bottle is framed by an abstract floral form inspired by the marula fruit and its surrounding landscape, while a circular arrangement of elephants creates a distinctive central emblem. Fine gold detailing adds a sense of craft and premium character without making the identity feel overly traditional.

The result is a direction that feels bold, celebratory and contemporary while remaining connected to Amarula’s African roots.

Heritage in Full Colour

The second direction explores a completely different personality. Red, deep blue and golden yellow create a bolder, more graphic identity inspired by the warmth, energy and visual richness I associate with South Africa.

Here, the elephant motif becomes more rhythmic and pattern-led, appearing as a continuous decorative band across both the bottle and packaging. The typography takes on a more expressive character, giving the design a sense of nostalgia while still feeling intentionally contemporary.

Where the first direction feels organic and expressive, this route is structured, vibrant and heritage-led.

A Story Beyond the Label

The outer packaging offered an opportunity to tell more of the story that initially made me interested in Amarula.

Opening the box reveals information about the marula fruit, its connection to the African landscape and the elephants associated with its harvest, allowing the packaging to become part of the brand experience rather than simply a protective shell.

This direction combines storytelling, pattern and colour to create something that feels collectible and rooted in place.

One Story, Two Expressions

Rather than approaching the project with a predetermined aesthetic, I used the rebrand as an opportunity to explore how the same brand story could lead to two distinctly different identities.

The pink and brown direction interprets Amarula through a more contemporary, organic and expressive lens, while the red, blue and yellow direction leans into graphic pattern, warmth and heritage.

Both are connected by the same foundation: the marula fruit, the elephant and the landscape that gives Amarula its story.

This project became an exploration of how a heritage brand can evolve visually without losing the cultural narrative that makes it distinctive in the first place.
